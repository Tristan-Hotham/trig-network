# Cover note: The trig pillar network

## Suitable journals with no charge to the author (checked 2026)

Each journal below publishes without any charge to the author. For the two Taylor and Francis journals, open access is an optional extra that carries a fee; the standard route is free. Word limits are given only where a journal's own pages state one; the Taylor and Francis instructions pages could not be loaded from this workspace, so recheck both before submitting.

1. **Survey Review** (Taylor and Francis, for Survey Review Ltd). Scope covers positioning and measurement, engineering surveying and spatial information, and it carried much of the later literature on the British triangulation (for example Greaves 2004 on OSTN02). APC: none on the standard subscription route. It is a hybrid journal with optional paid open access, listed at EUR 2,895 by the Cirad journal guide (updated May 2023). Word limit: not stated on the pages reached (surveyreview.org points to the Taylor and Francis "Instructions for authors", which did not load). The paper is about 7,200 words before the references, including tables and captions. Best fit for the geodetic framing and the terrain-model validation.
2. **The Cartographic Journal** (Taylor and Francis, for the British Cartographic Society). Publishes history of cartography and surveying alongside cartographic methods. APC: none on the standard route; the publisher's journal page says authors "can choose to publish gold open access", which carries a fee. Double-anonymised peer review by at least two referees. Word limit: not stated on the society's page or the publisher's page reached; check the instructions on Editorial Manager. Suits a version that leads with the network map and the history of the Retriangulation.
3. **Cartographic Perspectives** (North American Cartographic Information Society). Platinum (diamond) open access: "no article processing charges (APC)", production costs met by NACIS; default licence CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, CC BY on request. Word limit: none stated for peer-reviewed articles; the abstract must be 250 words or fewer (this paper's abstract is 250 words). A good open option, though its readership is more North American and design-focused.

## Summary of the contribution (for a cover letter)

The Retriangulation of Great Britain (from 1936) is well documented as history but has not, as far as I can find, been measured as a whole from the Ordnance Survey's own legacy station list. This paper groups the list's 31,521 mark records into 24,433 stations, including 6,573 pillars, and tests, in an analysis pre-specified in a written plan, the network's spacing, regularity, construction dates, survival and its defining design rule: that neighbouring stations are intervisible. Over an open terrain model with curvature and refraction, and using the surveyed station heights, 95.0% of nearest-neighbour primary lines are clear (95% CI 91.5 to 97.1), against 16.6% for the same lines shifted 1 km and 10.9% for random land pairs. With the terrain model's own heights at the ends, the share falls to 67.1%, still far above the placebos. Of the 12 failures, 8 involve a structure whose height the list omits and 3 a station observed from a steel tower; one is unexplained. The comparison also shows that the zoom 9 terrain model sits a median 22 m below surveyed pillar heights, a useful validation of a widely used open product. Data and code are released openly.
